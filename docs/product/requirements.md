## Confirmed scoring, scorecard, and accessibility decisions

- Players enter a positive game margin from 1 through 121 using a large, high-contrast on-screen number keypad. The normal keyboard remains available on desktop. The winner/loser choice determines whether the margin is recorded in Plus Points or Minus Points.
- The scorecard mirrors the familiar paper-card layout. It contains Game Number, Game Points, Plus Points, Minus Points, opponent first and last name, and permanent Verification ID # (for example, `A-7`). Opponent initials are removed. The player's current Table/Seat is separate, changes by game as seating rotates, and is shown on the score-entry/review context.
- A normal win earns 2 game points; a loss earns 0; any skunk-level result earns 3. The official ACC rule is a skunk at a win of 31 or more points. Single/double/triple skunk icons are an optional player-facing, non-official visual distinction and never alter official scoring or records.
- Current ACC qualifying order is game points, games won, net point spread, plus points, head-to-head (when available), then a one-game playoff. Minus points support net-spread calculation and cross-checking but are not an additional tie-breaker.
- Cross checkers may correct paper or digital cards other than their own. Corrections use a signed plus/minus control, preserve the prior verification, prior value, editor, and timestamp, and recalculate dependent totals immediately by default without player reconfirmation. A reason is available and optional by default; tournament directors can require a reason and/or an additional cross-checker or director approval, in which case the correction remains pending. Corrections to published results create a new result version.
- Directors and co-directors can assign an already-linked account in their own tournament as a cross-checker. The pilot workflow is assignment-only, audited, forbids self-assignment and director/co-director targets, and grants no authority in any other tournament. General role editing and revocation are deferred until an equally explicit evidence-preserving lifecycle is designed.
- The judge experience includes an in-app searchable quick reference and a link to the dated official ACC rulebook. Cached offline access is preferred but not required.
- Score Entry presents the tournament visually as its name followed by its local date in `MM-DD-YYYY` format (for example, `Topaz 01-27-2026`), with city and event as separate context; internal UUIDs and administrative pilot references are hidden. It presents `Game Result`, `Game Winner:`, and the large Spread Points keypad on one screen. Once both values are present, it previews both cards: winner/loser wording, each player’s Game Points, and reciprocal signed Spread Points. The appropriate player-facing skunk aid appears alongside the entered spread.
- The digital scorecard keeps the familiar 12-game paper-card hierarchy without copying it exactly: player name followed by ACC number, permanent `ID #` in the card header, grouped `Game` (#/Points), grouped `Spread Points` (+/-), `Opponent` (Name), and `Verification` (ID #). Its grouped header and totals remain fixed while game rows scroll. It displays Games Won, Net Spread Points, and a top-level verification state, but does not display initials, a loss-count field, or `Checked by` to the player.
- After registration closes and seating is assigned, digital players receive their initial Table/Seat in the app and their permanent tournament verification ID. The director, co-director, and authorized administrative staff need a printable/viewable seating list for both digital and paper players, with name search and sorting by first name, last name, scorecard type, or current Table/Seat. Directors configure the table and seats-per-table capacity before assignments are published. SMS delivery is a future optional notification channel only after consent, a provider, and message-delivery/error requirements are approved; it is not an assumed fallback.
- Players & Check-In provides a quick player-name search that returns the current check-in status and scorecard type without changing the roster. A no-match result is explicit. Search remains tournament-scoped and does not expose the private roster to players or outsiders.
- The format-review prototype presents a `Current Game Results` screen and a separate `Review Current Game Result` screen. The review screen is a player-facing confirmation step; production submission continues to require the previously specified independent two-player verification workflow and must never treat a single review as authoritative.
- An eligible cross checker will be able to photograph or upload a paper scorecard and receive an editable, confidence-labelled **reviewable digital transcription** tied to that game and permanent verification ID. A reviewed paper/digital match can complete paper-evidence comparison only when the digital result was already server-verified. A paper/paper game requires two separately captured and reviewed cards. Exact scan comparisons reduce the exception queue, but neither scan, OCR, nor staff transcription alone can verify a game, change a scorecard, standings, or results. The queue must still surface unreadable, uncertain, mismatched, missing-counterpart, unverified, and audit-sampled cards. [Paper scorecard capture contract](../decisions/2026-09-10-paper-scorecard-capture-contract.md)

## Confirmed event, flyer, results, and financial decisions

- Every Main, Consolation, and Satellite has an independent manual play lifecycle: **Preparing → Ready to Start → In Progress → Completed → Finalized**. Registration closure, seating publication, schedule publication, and Start Play remain separate. Only a director/co-director may start an event, against exact roster and schedule versions; all online, offline, paper-card, confirmation, and recovery score writes fail closed before that event starts. Existing score-bearing events are treated as started, while scoreless published schedules remain Ready to Start.
- The active leaderboard is named **Live Preliminary Standings**. It uses only verified or officially corrected results, refreshes every ten seconds and after accepted local results, and displays last-updated, offline/stale, resolved-game, and unresolved-tie state. Final results remain separate.
- Main, Consolation, and every Satellite may each configure the four customary Side Pools. Side Pools are separate from the existing two Q Pools. Definitions, elections, receipts, payouts, voids, and reconciliation evidence are versioned and cent-conserving.
- Event participation supports audited absent, substituted, withdrawn, disqualified, and reinstated states plus source-backed late/missed-player, first-sit-out makeup, early-departure, final-game substitute, excluded-extra-game, and playoff-absence operations. Current-game forfeits are game-specific 2/+10 records and never manufacture player submissions or blanket future wins. Two-games-per-opponent formats never infer the second result. Reinstatement affects future participation; existing authoritative forfeits change only through a separate audited correction. Every schedule amendment stores the complete prior/proposed schedule, affected games, rule basis, reason, actor, time, receipt, and proof that unaffected assignments are unchanged.
- Future doubles/team data stores a team separately from all individual members, ACC numbers, accounts, fees, pool elections, and contributions. A shared team display name uses both full names. Digital team scoring remains disabled for the October Standard Singles pilot until team-specific scoring, confirmation, seating, pool, and reporting fixtures pass.
- Every configured Satellite has a result destination for winners, cashing positions, payouts, Q/Side Pools, and special hands. Satellite results have no MRPs and never qualify a player for Main or Consolation. Cashing scorecards require cross-check, records are retained at least twelve months, and ACC reporting is director-reviewed rather than automatically submitted.

- The September 18 director-onboarding target for the October 3 supervised pilot is limited to Standard Singles digital scoring. The must-work scope is tournament/event/Q-pool setup; registration/import, roster, manual payment status and check-in; registration closure, initial seating and a validated director-entered/imported schedule when approved automatic rotation is unavailable; two independent submissions plus two distinct eligible confirmations; verified scorecards; durable offline replay; manual paper evidence, dispute status/non-self resolution and cross-check/correction; results/qualifiers/approved MRP and Q-pool outputs; and complete financial reconciliation. Production Rulebook/quick-reference integration, the rich Judge Desk interface, digital team scoring, flyer creation/import, online payments, SMS, machine OCR, and automatic ACC submission are deferred and must not block the required manual paths. Private paper-card photography/storage is now part of the approved manual evidence workflow; OCR activation remains a separately gated future capability. The demonstration may retain a reference-only Rulebook preview that is not release evidence. Main, Consolation, and Satellites remain separate events under one tournament. [Pilot minimum decision](../decisions/2026-09-11-september-18-october-pilot-minimum.md); [provider activation plan](../operations/PROVIDER_ACTIVATION_PLAN.md).

- Rename the existing Reserve Fee label to **ACC Sanctioning Fee**; its amount behavior is otherwise unchanged.
- Flyer creation supports Main, Consolation (with the familiar display name "Consy"), and a dedicated Satellite Events section. Directors can select a standard event type or create a custom event, with name, date/time, format, game count, fees, pools, payout/qualifying details, eligibility notes, and a Muggins-in-effect disclosure.
- **Set Up Tournament** is the single canonical source for tournament, director, venue, event, fee, Q-pool, and payout information. It offers guided entry based on the observed ACC sanctioning-request fields. A later existing-flyer upload may provide assisted extraction followed by director confirmation, but flyer import is deferred for the October 3 pilot. Approved setup data feeds **Tournament Events and Flyer**, seating, results, finance, and a director-assisted draft worksheet aligned to observed ACC fields; it is not an ACC-approved import or submission contract. It must not submit into the ACC portal unless the ACC provides a documented, authorized API or import contract.
- Main and Consolation support up to two Q-pools with the ACC portal's current equal, equal-with-top-qualifier-double, and graduated payout options. Exact payout and rounding fixtures remain subject to current ACC approval.
- A post-event Results page is visible by default to signed-in app users with results-view permission. It separates **Event/Playoff Results** (winner, runner-up, other paid playoff placements, and prize amounts) from **Qualification Results** (qualifiers in their original highest-to-lowest qualifying order, with MRP and Q-pool fields). Playoff finish never rewrites qualifying rank. The High Non-Qualifier is the first ranked player outside the cutoff and appears as a separately labeled row immediately after the last qualifier; it is not a playoff placement. Before playoffs finish, the Qualification Preview shows the qualification ranking/cutoff and provisional High Non-Qualifier but no winner or runner-up. The high non-qualifier uses a source-backed approved fixture and includes the final qualifier tie-playoff loser where applicable. After manual reconciliation is finalized, every authorized tournament role can download a final PDF generated from the exact latest qualification, playoff, settlement, and finalization versions; the control is absent before that point. The PDF includes reviewed playoff prizes, qualifying ranks, director-transcribed MRP and Q-pool/other award amounts, and the separately labeled High Non-Qualifier immediately after the qualifier list. It is not an automatic ACC submission. Canadian Doubles and other configured team events use a team-scorecard workflow only after approved team-event ruleset fixtures and verification controls exist. Results feed financial reconciliation and produce an internal, versioned director-assisted export (`acc-results-v1`) for manual portal submission; it is not an ACC portal import format. Anonymous access and automatic ACC submission require separate approval/contracts. See [qualification and playoff-result separation](../decisions/2026-09-10-qualification-and-playoff-result-separation.md).
